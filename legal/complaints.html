How We Keep Our Shit Together

Complaints
& Concerns.

If we've got something wrong, you're allowed to say so.

Problems don't improve by pretending they aren't there.

Veterans Backbone is currently being established and is not yet a registered charity or an operational support service.

We can receive general concerns about the project and its website during development.

Our wider complaints, whistleblowing and safeguarding procedures are being prepared for formal approval and implementation before services launch.

Our approach

Complaints aren't betrayal.

We want people to feel able to raise genuine concerns without being treated badly for speaking up.

Nobody should have to pretend something is fine because challenging it might make things awkward.

The complaints and concerns framework we're developing is based on:

  • Listening without unnecessary judgement
  • Taking concerns seriously
  • Responding fairly and proportionately
  • Protecting people from retaliation
  • Using appropriate human oversight
  • Keeping suitable records
  • Learning when something goes wrong

The intention is to make accountability part of how VB operates, not just something mentioned in an impressive policy.

If we're wrong, we'd rather know.

Current arrangements

What can you raise right now?

Veterans Backbone is not providing veteran support, running events, operating licensed sites or accepting individual referrals.

But the project is active in the sense that we're developing its website, governance and plans.

You can therefore contact us about concerns involving the current project.

  • Inaccurate or misleading website information
  • Accessibility barriers on the website
  • Privacy or personal information concerns
  • Project communications or correspondence
  • Conduct by someone representing the project
  • Other concerns directly connected with current activities

We're not going to pretend there is already a fully staffed complaints team.

At present, the founder manages the general project email.

Our independent escalation and formal complaints review arrangements still need to be established before operational launch.

No imaginary departments. No imaginary services.

When our services begin

Accountability will need to grow with us.

Our developing complaints framework is intended to cover future operational activities, including:

  • Support provided to veterans
  • Decisions made about services
  • Staff, volunteer and representative conduct
  • Community and RV Crew activities
  • Events and other programmes
  • Safeguarding, safety or governance concerns
  • Any future authorised affiliated sites

These are planned areas of responsibility.

They are not evidence that those services, teams or locations already exist.

The formal process will need clear responsibilities, appropriate escalation, decision recording and routes for review.

Sometimes it's simple

Not every problem needs seventeen forms.

Some issues can be resolved through a simple explanation, correction or conversation.

If you've noticed a mistake on the website, for example, you might simply want us to fix it.

That's fine.

But you shouldn't have to attempt informal resolution first if a concern is serious, involves safeguarding or you don't feel comfortable doing so.

The appropriate response depends on the nature and seriousness of the issue.

Raising a complaint

Tell us what happened.

For concerns directly relating to the current Veterans Backbone project, please contact:

veteransbackbone@gmail.com

It helps if you can briefly explain:

  • What the concern is about
  • When it happened, if relevant
  • Which part of the website or project is involved
  • What you would like us to consider
  • How we can contact you if a response is needed

You don't need legal terminology, a perfect email or a ten-page statement.

Please do not send medical records, confidential case files, identity documents or detailed sensitive disclosures through the general project email.

If further information is needed, we will need to consider an appropriate way of handling it.

What happens next?

Somebody needs to actually look at it.

Concerns should be considered according to their nature, urgency and seriousness.

During the current development stage, our aim is to acknowledge and respond to project correspondence as soon as reasonably practicable.

We haven't yet adopted a formal complaints response timetable, so we won't invent deadlines that we cannot guarantee.

The intended formal process will require an appropriate person to consider:

  • What has been reported
  • Whether anybody may be at risk
  • Who has authority to consider the matter
  • Whether a conflict of interest exists
  • Whether more information is required
  • Whether safeguarding or another procedure applies
  • What response or action is appropriate

Significant matters should be recorded appropriately.

Where possible and appropriate, the person who raised the concern should be informed of the outcome, subject to confidentiality and legal restrictions.

Fairness

Everybody involved is still a human.

A concern shouldn't automatically be dismissed because it is uncomfortable.

Equally, an allegation shouldn't automatically be treated as proven simply because somebody has raised it.

Our intended approach involves fair, proportionate review based on relevant information.

Conflicts of interest must be recognised and managed.

Where appropriate, somebody complained about should have a fair opportunity to respond.

The seriousness of a concern should determine the appropriate safeguards, not the popularity or status of the person involved.

Fairness goes both ways.

What if the concern is about leadership?

Nobody should be above challenge.

A complaint involving someone responsible for reviewing complaints creates an obvious conflict of interest.

Our proposed governance arrangements require those conflicts to be identified and managed appropriately.

The project is currently pre-registration, and the general project inbox is managed by the founder.

It is therefore not an independent complaints channel.

A suitable independent escalation route will need to be established as the organisation's governance is formally put into operation.

If your concern relates to a potential crime, serious risk or data protection breach, you can approach the relevant statutory authority or regulator directly.

You do not need to wait for our internal arrangements to be completed where an external reporting route is appropriate.

Possible outcomes

Fix what needs fixing.

Depending on the circumstances and available authority, responding to a complaint could involve:

  • Providing an explanation or clarification
  • Correcting inaccurate information
  • Offering an appropriate apology
  • Reviewing how something was handled
  • Changing a procedure or communication
  • Seeking independent advice where appropriate
  • Considering safeguarding or legal action
  • Referring a serious matter to an appropriate authority
  • Taking no further action where this is justified

Not every complaint will result in the outcome somebody hoped for.

But the reason for a decision should be considered properly and communicated where appropriate.

Safeguarding concerns

Some things can't wait for the usual process.

A concern involving abuse, neglect, exploitation, immediate danger or serious harm may require a safeguarding or emergency response.

If somebody is in immediate danger, call 999.

For non-emergency adult safeguarding concerns, contact the appropriate local authority safeguarding service or another established specialist service.

Veterans Backbone does not currently provide individual safeguarding assessments, crisis support or an emergency response service.

Please don't rely on our project inbox for urgent assistance.

Safeguarding & Standards
Serious wrongdoing

Whistleblowing isn't being difficult.

Our developing governance framework includes arrangements for raising serious concerns about wrongdoing.

Examples may include:

  • Serious safeguarding failures
  • Suspected unlawful conduct
  • Misuse of funds or resources
  • Serious governance breaches
  • Deliberate concealment of significant wrongdoing

The intended procedures support appropriate confidentiality, escalation and protection against retaliation.

Legal whistleblowing protections depend on the circumstances and whether the relevant legal conditions are met.

We won't promise absolute anonymity or suggest that an ordinary email is an anonymous reporting channel.

Specific reporting roles and independent escalation arrangements will need to be established before operational launch.

Speaking up should never be the problem.

Privacy

Complaints aren't public entertainment.

Information about complaints and concerns should be treated with appropriate care.

Access should be limited to those with a legitimate need to consider the matter.

Absolute confidentiality cannot always be guaranteed.

There may be lawful reasons to share relevant information, including safeguarding, statutory reporting or fair consideration of an allegation.

Any information sharing should be necessary, lawful and proportionate.

Our Privacy & Data page explains the wider principles for personal information handling.

Privacy & Data
Future affiliated sites

Local autonomy still comes with standards.

Veterans Backbone does not currently operate licensed or affiliated sites.

Any future site arrangements will need clear complaints responsibilities and escalation routes.

Our proposed model allows for local decision-making within agreed safeguarding, governance and accountability requirements.

Serious concerns may need escalation beyond local management where appropriate.

The actual authority to review or intervene will depend on lawful agreements and the arrangements put in place.

Local independence doesn't mean nobody is accountable.

What if you disagree?

Say so.

If you believe a concern hasn't been considered fairly, you should be able to explain why.

Our formal complaints framework will need to include appropriate routes for escalating or reviewing decisions.

Wherever reasonably possible, review arrangements should avoid conflicts of interest.

Those arrangements are not yet fully operational.

Nothing in this page prevents somebody from approaching an appropriate external body where they are entitled to do so.

One boundary

Complaining is fine. Abuse isn't.

Raising a complaint, disagreeing or feeling angry doesn't automatically make somebody unreasonable.

People can be upset, frustrated or experiencing significant challenges.

That must be considered fairly, including any relevant accessibility or communication needs.

But threatening, discriminatory, deliberately harassing or abusive behaviour may require proportionate boundaries.

Those boundaries should not be used to avoid examining genuine complaints.

Learn from it

Otherwise what's the bloody point?

Significant concerns, decisions and resulting actions should be recorded appropriately.

Records should be kept securely and only for as long as necessary under the relevant retention rules.

Where several concerns reveal a repeated problem, that pattern should be considered.

The aim isn't simply to close a complaint and move on.

It's to recognise what went wrong and decide whether something needs to change.

These learning and oversight arrangements will form part of the organisation's future governance processes.

Don't hide the problem. Fix the bloody problem.
