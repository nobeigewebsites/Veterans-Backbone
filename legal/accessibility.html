How We Keep Our Shit Together

Accessibility.

People shouldn't have to fight the website before they can even find the information they need.

Remove barriers. Don't create new ones.

Veterans Backbone is currently being established, and this website is still under development.

We are not yet a registered charity, and our veteran support services have not launched.

This page explains what we're aiming for, what we're doing during development and how you can tell us if something isn't accessible.

Our approach

Accessibility is part of doing things properly.

Veterans Backbone wants its website, information and future services to be accessible to as many people as reasonably possible.

People access information differently. Disability, illness, technology, communication needs and individual circumstances can all affect someone's experience.

Accessibility shouldn't be something we remember at the very end because somebody asks an awkward question.

It needs to be considered while the organisation and its services are being developed.

Different needs don't make somebody difficult.

The website

We're trying to make this thing usable.

The public Veterans Backbone website is being built with accessibility in mind.

Our development priorities include:

  • Clear page headings and structure
  • Readable text and suitable colour contrast
  • Alternative text for meaningful images
  • Keyboard-accessible navigation and controls
  • Visible keyboard focus indicators
  • Descriptive links and buttons
  • Responsive layouts for different devices
  • Forms with understandable labels and instructions
  • Clear error messages where forms are used
  • Content that can be understood without unnecessary jargon

These are development aims, not a claim that every page has already passed a full accessibility assessment.

We intend to review the website against relevant accessibility guidance, including the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, and address identified issues.

We have not yet verified full conformance with a particular WCAG level.

Different devices

Not everybody is sitting at a massive monitor.

The website is intended to work on desktop computers, laptops, tablets and mobile phones.

Pages should adjust to different screen sizes without requiring unnecessary horizontal scrolling or constant zooming.

We'll need to keep checking layouts as pages and features are changed or added.

A page looking lovely on one laptop doesn't prove it works for everybody else.

Assistive technology

The website shouldn't assume everyone uses it the same way.

Some visitors use accessibility tools or assistive technology, including:

  • Screen readers
  • Keyboard-only navigation
  • Screen magnification
  • Browser zoom
  • Voice control
  • Operating-system accessibility settings

We aim to make the website compatible with these methods wherever reasonably possible.

That includes considering how navigation menus, buttons, login features and other interactive elements behave.

Any compatibility problems found during testing will need to be reviewed rather than ignored.

Language matters too

Plain English beats impressive waffle.

Accessibility isn't only about colours, screens and buttons.

Information can be technically accessible and still be almost impossible to understand.

We aim to explain things in straightforward language and avoid unnecessary jargon.

Sometimes legal, technical or specialist terms cannot reasonably be avoided.

When that happens, we should explain what the bloody hell those terms actually mean.

Future support services

Accessibility doesn't stop at the website.

Veterans Backbone's planned community-based support service will need to consider people's individual communication and accessibility requirements.

That could include:

  • Explaining information in a different way
  • Allowing additional time where appropriate
  • Considering alternative communication methods
  • Identifying reasonable adjustments
  • Recognising barriers linked to disability or health
  • Reviewing arrangements when needs change

Actual arrangements will depend on the services established, individual circumstances and applicable legal duties.

This is our intended approach. We are not currently providing individual support or accepting referrals.

Work with the person. Not an imaginary average human.

Future events & activities

Tell us what you need when there's something to attend.

We hope to introduce appropriate community activities and events as Veterans Backbone develops.

Any future event arrangements will need to consider accessibility, suitable venues, communication needs and reasonable adjustments.

Organisers should provide clear information so that people can understand what to expect.

Where a barrier cannot reasonably be removed, alternative arrangements should be considered where possible.

No Veterans Backbone events or RV Crew activities are currently operational.

Documents & information

A PDF isn't automatically accessible because it opens.

As Veterans Backbone develops, we may publish governance information, policies, guides, workbooks and other resources.

Where reasonably possible, important information should be made available in formats people can actually use.

That may include accessible web pages, appropriately structured documents or suitable alternative formats.

If a document we have published is difficult for you to access, please contact us.

We can discuss whether a suitable alternative can be provided.

Some governance documents are still working drafts and are not yet approved public policies.

Third-party features

Accessibility matters there too.

Parts of the website may use external platforms, including hosting and member authentication.

The member login currently uses Clerk authentication technology.

Accessibility issues can sometimes arise within features supplied by third-party providers.

We will need to consider those features as part of the website's accessibility review.

Using an external service doesn't mean we should ignore problems with accessing it.

We're not claiming perfection

Accessibility is ongoing.

The website is still under construction.

We have not completed a verified, comprehensive accessibility audit covering every page, feature and third-party tool.

Some elements may therefore present barriers that have not yet been identified or resolved.

As problems are discovered, our aim is to understand them and make reasonable improvements.

We won't claim the website is fully accessible simply because it looks good.

Looking pretty isn't the same as working properly.

Found a problem?

Please tell us.

If something on the Veterans Backbone website is difficult or impossible for you to access, we'd like to know.

It helps if you can tell us:

  • Which page or feature caused difficulty
  • What happened when you tried to use it
  • What device or browser you were using, if known
  • What accessibility tool you were using, if relevant
  • What would make the information more accessible

You don't need to know any technical terminology to report a problem.

Email: veteransbackbone@gmail.com

Please avoid sending sensitive health information or confidential personal documents through the general project inbox.

Still not sorted?

You can raise a concern.

If you've reported an accessibility problem and believe it has not been handled properly, you're welcome to raise a complaint.

Veterans Backbone is still establishing its formal governance arrangements, so we won't pretend there's already a fully staffed complaints department.

But concerns about the current project and website should still be taken seriously.
