So... what actually is it?

The bit where people stop
being referrals.

The RV Crew is the community we're planning to build alongside Veterans Backbone.

The aim is a place where friendships can happen, connection can continue beyond the boring paperwork and perfectly sensible conversations have a tendency to descend into complete chaos.

It won't be another appointment or another form. The idea is to create somewhere people can belong.

Good people. Good laughs. Good backup.

The RV Crew Charter

We have
rules.

Well...

Technically we have one.

Don't be a dick.

That's it.

Respect people. Have each other's backs. Don't judge somebody because their life looks different to yours. Don't deliberately make somebody else's day harder.

You don't have to agree with everybody. You don't have to like everybody. You definitely don't have to behave like you're attending a corporate team-building retreat.

Just don't be a dick.

Need further clarification?
You may already be breaking Rule #1.

Nobody Gets Left Behind

Support doesn't finish because
one problem did.

Most support tends to work like this:

Problem identified.
Support provided.
Case closed.

Life doesn't work like that.

Housing problems can create financial stress. Financial stress can affect wellbeing. Wellbeing can affect employment.

Problems travel in packs.

That's why solving one issue doesn't mean the rest of somebody's life magically behaves itself.

Important distinction

We're not building
dependency.

Nobody is here to hold your hand forever.

The whole point is to help build:

Stability

Confidence

Independence

Purpose

Connection

Support changes as you grow.

Because obviously

Sometimes life throws
another grenade.

You can be doing brilliantly.

Then life happens.

A relationship ends. Your health wobbles. You lose a job. A family problem appears from nowhere.

Our proposed Nobody Gets Left Behind approach aims to make future reconnection to appropriate support easier, rather than starting from square one every time life punches you in the face.

Wobbling isn't failing.

The bottom line

We don't measure success by how quickly
we close a case.

Once operational, we hope to judge success by lasting progress rather than how fast a case gets closed.

Sometimes the biggest difference isn't fixing one problem.

It's knowing somebody still gives a shit afterwards.
